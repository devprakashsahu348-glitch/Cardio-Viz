See which coronary artery is at risk, and why.

CardioViz turns 16 routine clinical inputs into a risk estimate for overall coronary artery disease and for the three main vessels, then colours each artery on a rotating 3D heart.

Open the demo How it works

How it works

  1. Enter clinical data. Age, sex, blood pressure, pulse, chest pain type, diabetes, hypertension, LDL, HDL, triglycerides, fasting glucose, ST elevation, T inversion, ejection fraction, region with RWMA and BMI.
  2. Four models predict. One regularised logistic regression each for overall CAD, LAD, LCX and RCA. Catheterisation results are never used as inputs.
  3. The heart shows the result. Each artery turns from green (low) to red (high) by predicted probability. Click an artery to select it.
  4. SHAP bars explain it. A linear model gives exact contributions in log-odds, so every input shows how much it pushed the risk up or down.

Safety and limits

Clinical safety notice. Predictions are for decision support and education only. They are not a diagnosis and do not replace diagnostic imaging or a clinician's judgement.

The model is trained on the Z-Alizadeh Sani dataset (UCI Machine Learning Repository): a small, single-centre cohort with no external validation. The heart and arteries are schematic, not patient-specific anatomy. Region with RWMA is a clinical input, not a lesion map.

Frequently asked questions

Is CardioViz a medical device?

No. It is a research prototype and must not be used to diagnose, treat or rule out any condition.

Does the demo send my inputs anywhere?

Not unless you want it to. Predictions are calculated in your browser. Values are sent to our server only if you are logged in and press Save this prediction, and you can delete them any time. The contact form also sends what you type to our server.

Is it free?

Yes. Using CardioViz costs nothing. See the terms for how any future paid features would work.

Can I use my own dataset?

Not in this version. The demo uses illustrative weights while validated models are being built.
